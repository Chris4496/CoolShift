## 1 · Title

> Cool Shift. Small actions, everyday value.
>
> It is a personal energy assistant for Hong Kong households. It uses what CLP
> already owns — the smart meters, the tariffs, the rewards programme, the
> partners — and turns them into one daily journey.
>
> The point of it is simple. Give 2.9 million households a reason to move
> electricity off the evening peak.

*≈25 секунд. Не читай подзаголовок со слайда вслух — он и так виден.*

## 2 · The problem, for CLP

> Look at this strip. It is one day.
>
> The orange part is CLP's own on-peak window. Four in the afternoon to eleven
> at night. Seven hours.
>
> Hong Kong turns on its air conditioning in those same hours, every evening,
> all summer. Air conditioning is about 38% of residential electricity.
>
> So the grid gets sized for that evening. Peak demand last year was 7,336
> megawatts. The approved capital plan for 2024 to 2028 is 52.9 billion dollars,
> and a large part of it is transmission and distribution that exists to carry
> the busiest hour of the day.
>
> That capacity is paid for all year and used hard for a few weeks.

*≈45 секунд. Покажи рукой на оранжевую полосу, прежде чем назвать цифры.
Цифра 52.9 млрд — главная. Сделай паузу после неё.*

## 3 · The problem, for the customer

> Now the same problem from the kitchen.
>
> On the left is what 2.9 million households are on today. A block tariff, read
> every two months. The rate goes up with how much you use. The hour you use it
> never enters the calculation.
>
> Which means if I move my dryer to midnight, my bill is identical. I have done
> CLP a favour and I have paid for it with my evening.
>
> On the right is the exception. CLP launched a time-of-use tariff for EV
> charging in May last year. 137.5 cents on peak, 94.5 cents off peak. That is a
> 31% gap, and it is real money. But you need an electric car and a charger at
> home to see it.
>
> So for everybody else there is no signal. The grid needs the shift. The
> household gets nothing for it.
>
> That is the gap we are closing. Cool Shift builds the missing signal out of
> points and partner value. No tariff change, no regulatory approval.

*≈60 секунд. Это ключевой слайд. Здесь жюри должно услышать, что ты знаешь их
тарифную структуру лучше, чем от тебя ожидают.*

## 4 · Why now

> Three things became true this year.
>
> CLP finished replacing every meter at the end of 2025. 2.88 million smart
> meters. Every home now reports by the half hour. Before that there was simply
> nothing to personalise with.
>
> Second, electric vehicles. 131,700 on the road, and 71% of new private cars
> registered last year were electric. One car charging at seven in the evening
> adds roughly a whole household's daily electricity straight onto the peak.
>
> And third, the 2050 target is law now, not ambition.

*≈35 секунд.*

---

## 5 · The product

> Here is what it does, in four parts.
>
> Energy insights. A personal profile of the home, the bill broken down line by
> line, and three suggestions a day. Each one is a single action with a dollar
> figure attached.
>
> Smart home and e-mobility. Air conditioning scheduled against humidity, not
> just temperature. EV and e-bike charging moved into the off-peak window.
>
> Inclusive journeys. Cantonese, English and Mandarin with voice input. A
> large-text mode. A caregiver view so somebody can watch an elderly parent's
> usage. Budget alerts. And no smart-home hardware, because most households in
> Hong Kong do not have any.
>
> And the partner platform, which I will come back to.

*≈40 секунд. Не задерживайся — это слайд-оглавление, а не аргумент.*

## 7 · The status quo

> Before I show the partner model, I want to be fair about what CLP already
> does, because it already rewards saving.
>
> Renewable Energy Certificates. Open to everyone on paper, but they are sold
> from 100 units and paid in cash, so in practice the buyers are hotels and
> corporates. A family cannot realistically take part.
>
> Power Connect and Domeo points. You earn them for going paperless and paying
> by autopay, and you spend them on supermarket coupons and Asia Miles. The
> reward has nothing to do with energy.
>
> Energy-Saving Rewards. 200 points if this bill is lower than the same bill
> last year. It arrives up to two months after the behaviour, and it never tells
> you how to save, or when.
>
> And here is the app today. Every programme sits behind its own icon on the
> Discover tab. They do not talk to each other.
>
> All three pay you for using less electricity. None of them pays you for using
> it at a different hour. That is the thing the grid actually needs.

*≈55 секунд. Покажи на скриншот Discover, когда говоришь про иконки.
Тон уважительный — не критикуй, а показывай пробел.*

## 8 · The partnership proposal

> So this is what the points should buy.
>
> Air conditioner cleaning and servicing at a discount, offered at the moment
> the meter shows the unit's efficiency dropping.
>
> Vouchers at the local cha chaan teng, valid six to nine in the evening.
> Somebody eats out during the peak instead of cooking and cooling at home. The
> reward is the load shift.
>
> Fifty dollars off the monthly management fee, through the property manager.
> Or discounted home safety insurance.
>
> Fractional RECs. Households pool their points to buy certificates in the
> hundred-unit blocks the programme already sells. That opens a corporate
> product to families.
>
> And for EV owners, 15% off or bonus credits on off-peak charging sessions.
>
> Partners pay on results. And nothing on this list increases energy use. That
> is a hard rule.

*≈50 секунд.*

## 9 · Value proposition

> Four parties, one loop.
>
> Customers get a lower bill without giving up comfort, and three clear actions
> a day instead of a chart.
>
> CLP gets a lower evening peak against a 52.9 billion dollar capital plan,
> contact with the customer daily instead of every two months, and a new
> commission line.
>
> Partners reach a household at the moment of need and pay only when it works.
>
> And Hong Kong gets a flatter peak and lower emissions out of behaviour, with
> no new substation.

*≈35 секунд.*

## 10 · Responsible AI

> One discipline that runs through all of it.
>
> The savings are calculated. Deterministically, from meter data and the
> published tariff. The language model never produces a number. It explains the
> number, in the customer's language, and it answers questions like "why did my
> bill go up".
>
> So the AI does four things. It personalises, it optimises the timing, it
> explains, and it holds a conversation.
>
> And we judge it on kilowatt-hours shifted and dollars saved. Not on how much
> people chatted with it.

*≈35 секунд. Фраза «the language model never produces a number» — самая
важная в этом слайде. Скажи её медленно.*

## 11 · Pilot

> Twelve weeks, this summer.
>
> A thousand households with smart meters, in one or two estates. About a
> hundred EV owners. A group of elderly residents. Five to ten local partners.
> It runs inside the existing CLP app, so there is nothing for a customer to
> install.
>
> And a matched control group, which is the part that matters. Every number we
> report back is a difference against households that did not get the app. Not a
> before-and-after that a hot August could explain.
>
> We are looking for evening-peak consumption five to eight percent below
> control. Sixty percent of EV charging moved off peak. Forty percent weekly
> active users, thirty percent of suggestions acted on. And a verified average
> saving per household, in dollars.

*≈50 секунд. Про контрольную группу говори уверенно — это то, что отличает
проверяемое предложение от презентации.*

## 12 · Risks, scale, the ask

> Briefly, the risks.
>
> Privacy is opt-in, with data minimisation, and no household data goes to a
> partner.
>
> Wrong advice is handled by showing savings as ranges from deterministic
> calculations, with a human-reviewed library of suggestions.
>
> Health comes first. We never advise less cooling during a hot weather warning,
> or for a vulnerable household.
>
> And fatigue is handled by a hard limit of three suggestions a day.
>
> After the pilot, this goes to all smart-meter users in the app, then device
> control and an open partner API, then automated demand response. And in the
> long run, a household time-of-use tariff, with an app that has already taught
> people how to live on one.
>
> The ask is one thing. Approval to run the twelve-week pilot this summer.

*≈45 секунд. Последнюю фразу — глядя в зал, не на экран.*

## Q&A

**"Our residential tariff is not time-of-use. How does a customer save money by
shifting?"**
> They do not, on the tariff. That is exactly our starting point, and it is on
> slide three. A household saves on the tariff by using less, and the app does
> drive that. The reason to shift comes from the rewards layer and the partners,
> which is a lever you control without going to the regulator. The pilot is
> designed to measure whether that lever is strong enough. If it is, it is also
> the evidence base for a household time-of-use tariff later.

**"Why would anyone open this every day?"**
> Three suggestions, capped. A budget they set themselves. A building-level
> challenge. And rewards they can spend within walking distance. Four different
> return loops, and we measure weekly actives honestly in the pilot.

**"What if the AI gives wrong advice?"**
> It cannot invent a number. The savings come out of a rules engine over meter
> data and the published tariff. The model writes the sentence around the
> number. The suggestion library is human-reviewed, and we show ranges rather
> than false precision.

**"What does it cost to build?"**
> It runs inside the existing CLP app. The engine is rules over data you already
> collect. The language layer is an API integration. The real cost of the pilot
> is partner onboarding and measurement, not engineering.

**"Privacy?"**
> Opt-in consent, data minimisation, no household-level data to partners, and
> community figures anonymised by default.

**"What if people just use more electricity to earn points?"**
> No reward on the list can be earned by consuming more, and the cha chaan teng
> voucher only pays out during the peak window. We also report total consumption
> against control, not just the shift, so the pilot would catch it.
